# Competitive Landscape: Streak / Habit / Micro-Learning Apps

> Scope: apps built around streaks, daily habits, and micro-learning. Excludes social media and long-form course platforms.
> Data note: pricing and feature details below are compiled from public reporting and third-party review/aggregator sites as of September 2026, not verified against each company's own pricing page — treat exact prices as directional, since they vary by region, platform (iOS/Android/web), and ongoing promotions.

## 1. Duolingo

**Core features:** Gamified daily lessons across languages plus non-language tracks (Math, Music, Chess); leaderboards; an "energy" system (2026 rework) that rewards correct answers instead of penalizing mistakes; AI-powered "Video Call with Lily" for conversation practice; newer additions include Advanced Stories (reading) and DuoRadio (listening); Duolingo Score now integrates with LinkedIn profiles.

**Pricing model:** Free tier (ad-supported, limited by the energy system). Super Duolingo (~$13–17/month or ~$120/year) removes ads and energy limits, adds personalized practice and streak repair. Max (~$168/year) adds AI video-call roleplay, expanding to more courses. Family plans available (~$120–144/year for multiple accounts).

**Target customer:** Mass-market, casual learners across a broad age range; optimized for habit-building and fun over depth or credentialing.

**Post-week-1 engagement:** Daily streak is the central loop, reinforced by leaderboards/social competition and (paid) streak repair — a limited, once-available-per-30-days undo of a broken streak, distinct from a true comeback flow. The 2026 energy rework is an explicit move away from punishing mistakes toward rewarding consistency.

**Notable recent changes:** An April 2025 "AI-first" internal strategy announcement triggered user backlash and slowed growth; through 2025–2026 Duolingo has been walking back friction-based monetization (fewer ads/upsells that pushed users away) in favor of user-growth-first strategy, alongside the energy rework and expanding AI features from the Max tier down into Super.

## 2. Babbel

**Core features:** Linguist-built structured lessons with explicit grammar teaching and real-life conversational scenarios; Babbel Live (live classes with human teachers, group or private); an AI conversation partner (GPT-4o powered); per-user streak tracking.

**Pricing model:** No permanent free tier (trial-based). Roughly $14.99/month billed monthly, ~$9.99/month on a 6-month plan, ~$7.99/month (~$96/year) on the annual plan, and a lifetime option around $249–299. A single subscription covers all Babbel languages.

**Target customer:** Adults and professionals who want structured, substantive lessons and are willing to pay upfront — positioned against Duolingo's more casual, free-first audience.

**Post-week-1 engagement:** Streak tracking exists but plays a smaller role than at Duolingo; engagement leans more on live-class accountability and AI conversation practice than gamified pressure.

**Notable recent changes:** Continued build-out of the GPT-4o-powered AI conversation partner; recent bolt-on acquisitions (including a gamified vocabulary product) suggest Babbel is adding lighter, habit-style features on top of its structured core.

## 3. Elevate (The Mind Company)

**Core features:** 40+ short cognitive-training games spanning reading, writing, speaking, math, and memory; a "study area" with tips on public speaking, speed reading, and vocabulary; weekly performance reports; 150+ achievements/badges; a workout calendar; a leaderboard against other users; daily streak.

**Pricing model:** Free tier limited to 3 games/day with no skill selection. Paid tier roughly $9.99/month or ~$39.99/year, with a 7-day free trial.

**Target customer:** Adults seeking general cognitive and professional-communication skills (writing, speaking, math) rather than language learning specifically — a broader "sharpen your mind" positioning.

**Post-week-1 engagement:** Streak plus badges/achievements plus a leaderboard plus weekly reports that reflect progress back to the user. No streak-freeze or repair mechanism found in available sources — this may be a genuine gap in their product, or simply not well documented publicly.

**Notable recent changes:** Little recent public product news found — Elevate appears to have a lower public profile and slower visible iteration pace than Duolingo, Babbel, or Mimo.

## 4. Memrise

**Core features:** Native-speaker video clips and real-life phrases (rather than abstract drills); spaced-repetition review ("Difficult Words"); an "Immerse" video-context section; offline mode; customizable reminders; streak tracking.

**Pricing model:** Pro roughly $25/month or ~$62/year; a lifetime option (list price ~$330, frequently discounted to ~$100–130).

**Target customer:** Language learners who want authentic, native-context exposure over Duolingo-style gamified drilling — a slightly more serious, mid-market audience.

**Post-week-1 engagement:** Streaks with customizable reminders. No confirmed streak-freeze/repair feature found in available sources — flagged as an open data gap rather than a confirmed absence.

**Notable recent changes:** Public reporting suggests Memrise is at a strategic crossroads (potential acquisition or continued independent operation) rather than shipping major new features — no confirmed deal as of this research.

## 5. Mimo

**Core features:** Bite-sized (under 5 minutes) coding lessons in Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, HTML/CSS, and SQL; structured career paths (Web Development, Data Science); hands-on projects; certifications; AI-powered coding guidance and explanations; recently added an AI "Instance" feature for no-code app building.

**Pricing model:** Free tier; Pro around $9.99/month; a higher Max tier (reported between ~$25–40/month depending on source) bundling AI credits and full web access, with a 7-day trial.

**Target customer:** Beginner-to-intermediate self-taught coders and career-switchers — tech-curious professionals rather than computer-science students.

**Post-week-1 engagement:** Daily streak plus "streak shields" (a freeze-equivalent) plus XP and achievement badges plus a visual path showing progress. Reporting suggests Mimo's streak mechanic drives higher daily-active-user retention than most learning apps in its category.

**Notable recent changes:** Raised $8.5M in an equity round (August 2025); launched an AI-powered no-code "Instance" feature, extending beyond pure coding lessons into AI-assisted app building.

## White Space for Streakly

**Gap 1 — No competitor has a true post-break "comeback" experience, only pre-break insurance.** Duolingo's streak repair and Mimo's streak shields are both forms of insurance used to prevent or undo a break — limited-use, often paywalled, and framed as damage control. None of the five turns the moment after a full streak loss into a positive re-engagement moment (e.g., surfacing what the user already built, easing them back in with something short and specific, rather than resetting silently to zero). This is exactly the gap Streakly's own user research surfaced (Tom R.'s churn, the NPS "coach, not scorekeeper" feedback) — and no competitor here owns it.

**Gap 2 — No competitor appears to tailor streak protection to the fragile first-week window specifically.** Where protection features exist (Duolingo, Mimo), they're generic across tenure and often gated behind a paid tier — meaning brand-new, free-tier users get zero protection exactly when they're most likely to quit (per Streakly's own interview finding: anxiety starts by day 4, well before any habit has formed). None of these five apps appears to specifically de-risk the first-week experience by user lifecycle stage rather than by subscription tier. A week-1-specific safety net — independent of whether someone has paid — is unclaimed territory.
